Most of this you'll never see, and that's deliberate — you're paying for it to be handled, not to be understood. But people ask, so here it is plainly.

## The short version

Every week, work gets written from [[the-static-brain|your four documents]], a person checks it, and what's ready shows up in your morning message for you to approve or correct.

That's it. The rest is detail.

## The longer version

**Something gets read first.** New reviews you've had. What your competitors have changed. What's coming up seasonally in your trade. Nothing gets written from thin air — if we can't point at where a claim came from, it doesn't ship.

**Then it gets written** — from your four documents, not from a template and not from what worked for someone else's business. That's the whole reason the documents exist.

**Then a person reads it.** Every batch, before it reaches you. They check it sounds like you, that every claim traces back to something real, and that nothing contradicts what your documents say. Things get sent back. This is the step that takes the longest and it's the one we won't cut.

**Then it's packaged for you to decide on** — grouped so you can approve a batch with one tap rather than reading twelve separate things.

**Then it lands in your account.** Your CRM, your pipeline, your templates — the system you already run your business from, not one of ours. Email drafts land as **drafts**. Sequences load **paused**. → [[the-managed-service]]

**We never press send.** Your list, your consent, your sender, your call.

## What you actually do

One minute in the morning. → [[the-morning-brief]]

Approve, or reply with a voice note if something's off. → [[training-by-voice-note]]

Occasionally it stops and asks you a real question rather than a yes/no — a fork where either answer is defensible and it's your business, not ours. → [[decision-gates]]

## Why you don't need to know more than this

If you found yourself needing to understand how the work moves between steps, something has gone wrong with what you're paying for. The point of a managed service is that the machinery is our problem.

What's worth your attention is the part you hold: [[the-static-brain|your four documents]], [[the-alignment-map|the map of what still disagrees]], and the batch in front of you each morning.
