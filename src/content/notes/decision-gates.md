Most mornings the brief asks one thing: *approve the finished work.* Some mornings it asks something different: *which way do you want to go?*

That's a decision gate. The work hit a fork where your judgment changes what happens next — and instead of guessing, the system did the homework, narrowed it to two real options, told you which one it'd pick, and left you a one-tap answer.

## Why a gate, not a guess

A few calls are yours, not the system's:

- **Which niche to attack next** — you know med spas reply faster than dermatologists; the system might not yet.
- **Which angle to lead with** — two openers both tested well; you know which one fits the brand.
- **Whether to widen or hold** — push into a second city, or go deeper in the first.

On these, a wrong autonomous guess costs you a week. So the system stops at the fork and asks. It still does everything *around* the fork — you're choosing a direction, not staring at a blank screen.

## It's a tap, not a conversation

A gate is the same one-tap surface as an approval. Two options, a recommendation, you tap. If neither is right, a voice note sends it a third way ([[training-by-voice-note]]) — and that correction lands in [[the-static-brain]], so the same fork doesn't come back next month.

What a gate is **not**: a chat thread, a menu to navigate, a dashboard to log into. You never drive the system. It brings the decision to you, already narrowed.

## When gates fire — the morning, not the middle of the night

The work runs without you sitting over it, so a gate can't stall and wait on you — you're asleep. Instead the run proceeds as far as it safely can on every branch that doesn't need you, and stacks the fork for your morning tap. Nothing blocks except the one branch that genuinely needs your call.

So a gate costs you the same minute an approval does. You wake up to *"here's what's done, here's what's ready, and here's the one call only you can make."*

**Made concrete.** For U Med Spa, week three the brief carried a gate. The natural-results niche was tapped out — every nearby spa already pitched — so the system asked: *lead the next sweep with med-spa-adjacent (dermatology, wellness), where the same review language wins — or hold and go deeper on the current list? Recommend: adjacent. Your reviews say "natural" the same way theirs do.* One tap. The branch that didn't need a decision — refreshing this week's ads against new reviews — had already run.
