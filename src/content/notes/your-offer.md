*One of the [[the-static-brain|four documents]] written at [[rung-1-foundation-build|the Build]].*

**The question it answers:** what exactly are you selling, what does it cost, and why should anyone believe it's worth that?

## What's actually in it

- **What you sell** — the actual services, named the way you name them, in the order you'd want someone to buy them.
- **What's included and what isn't.** The second half matters more than the first. Most disputes and most stalled quotes come from a boundary nobody wrote down.
- **What it costs** — the real numbers, the ranges, what moves them, and what you'll never discount.
- **How you sequence it** — what someone buys first, what comes next, what you'd never sell to someone who isn't ready.
- **What proves it** — the guarantee, the warranty, the reference job, the numbers you can actually stand behind.
- **What you turn down.** Being clear about the work you don't take is one of the strongest trust signals a business has, and almost nobody publishes it.

## Why it matters

This is the file that stops your marketing writing cheques the business can't cash.

The failure isn't usually a lie. It's an old price, a service you quietly stopped offering, a package name you renamed two years ago that's still live on a landing page nobody has opened since. Every one of those costs you either a job or an argument.

It's also the file [[the-alignment-map|the alignment map]] leans on hardest. When we read your Google listing, your proposal and your ads against your four documents, the offer file is the one most of the contradictions turn out to be against — because the offer is the thing that changes most and gets updated least.

## What breaks without it

Four different versions of your pricing in circulation, a proposal that promises something the website doesn't mention, and a sales conversation that has to start by undoing what your own marketing said. You lose the job to someone whose story was simply consistent.

## How it gets sharper

Every time you change a price, add a service, drop one, or find yourself explaining the same exception for the third time — that's the file updating. On the subscription it stays current as the business actually changes, rather than as an annual chore nobody does.
