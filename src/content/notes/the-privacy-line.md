# The privacy line

Your brain is private. Your marketing is public. The system checks the line every time something goes out.

This week we audited every skill in the system — all 45 of them — against one question: *can private business context ever leak into a public channel?* Where the answer wasn't a hard no, we fixed it.

What the line protects:

- **Client and prospect names** stay in your files unless you've approved them for public use.
- **Pricing logic, unreleased offers, and internal decisions** inform the copy — they never appear in it.
- **Your frameworks** ([[the-static-brain]]) ship as positioning, never verbatim. The principles go public; the mechanisms stay yours.

How it works in practice:

- **Before anything publishes**, the system scans the content against the line and shows you exactly what will go out — every asset gets its own preview and yes/no, even when you approve a whole campaign at once.
- **Your website reads your brain but doesn't quote it.** What's on the page is written *from* your context, not copied out of it.
- **If something private slips into a draft**, it gets rewritten before you ever see the preview — and you're told what was removed.

## Why this matters

[[you-own-everything]] only means something if owning it includes controlling what's visible. The whole point of a private brain is that the system knows things your competitors — and the public internet — don't. A check that runs on every send is what keeps that true while the system publishes for you every day.
