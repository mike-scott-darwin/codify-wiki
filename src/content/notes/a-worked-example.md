# A worked example

Here's the [[station-1-snapshot|Snapshot]] run on a real business — **U Med Spa**, a med spa in Plano, TX with 1,000+ five-star reviews. Nobody asked them. We read their homepage, mined 36 of their public reviews across Yelp, TripAdvisor, and their own site, found the gap between what their headline says and what their customers say, and rebuilt their above-the-fold. Start to finish, unprompted — exactly what would land in their inbox.

## What they have today

Their headline leads with a volume claim — *"Most Trusted Med Spa in DFW · Over 1,000 5-star reviews."* The thing customers actually praise most — *subtle, natural, never-frozen* results and a *no-pressure* consult — is buried in small sub-text below it.

![U Med Spa homepage today](/umedspa-before-hero.png)

## What their customers actually say

Three gaps, each grounded in real, cited reviews:

1. **"Natural" is the #1 thing customers praise — and it's not in the headline.** *"They are cheerleaders for achieving a subtle, natural look."* (Amber H., Yelp) · *"I don't like the 'frozen' look… she made me feel very comfortable."* (Sanna H., Yelp)
2. **The no-pressure, explained-every-step, followed-up-after consult is invisible.** *"No pressure. Truly great to let me know what I should do."* (Debbie S.) · *"Explained the process… followed up via text post-treatment."* (Terry A., Yelp)
3. **"1,000 5-star" hides the two fixable complaints** — wait time and promo clarity. *"Sat for almost an hour in the lobby."* (D.A., Yelp) · *"The offer is only for registered members."* (Diana M., TripAdvisor)

## What we sent back

A live, interactive redline in *their* brand colors — the original above-the-fold next to four rewrites, each headline pulled from their customers' own words, each annotated with the *why* tied to a specific review. It could ship on their site tomorrow.
